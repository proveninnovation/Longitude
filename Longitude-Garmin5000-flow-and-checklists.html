Role

Sim use only — not for real flight. Checklist items are transcribed from Section XVII of the Model 700 Operators Guide, itself a simulator publication; the PF/PM split is a proposal until Nic agrees it.

G5000 detail. A row carrying the badge has Garmin entry detail behind it — tap the badge, or long-press the row. You get the real GTC Home screen with the button to press ringed, and the fields to fill on the page it opens.
The split that catches everyone: every flight-management page — Flight Plan, PROC, Direct To, PERF — is on the inboard MFD GTC. Nav source, minimums and timers are on the outboard PFD GTC, and that one is per side: yours does not change the other seat.

The crew layer. Every flow opens with a trigger — the event in the world that fires it. It belongs to neither seat. Inside some flows there is a parallel band showing what each of you does at the same time, converging on the checklist that closes the phase. A ATC PA GND pill marks a call that leaves the cockpit.
Four reference cards sit at the end — who owns what, radio and comms, monitoring calls, and the rejected takeoff. They are doctrine, not running items, so there is nothing to tick.

Provenance. Checklists: Cessna Citation Longitude Model 700 Operators Guide, Section XVII. Sim technique and FMA states: JayDee's MSFS checklist v1.32.3. Crew mechanics: Alpha Jet Charter and AeroJet CE-525 operator SOPs, and the CitationMax Innsbruck video — same type, same avionics, real crew.

The crew layer — triggers, parallel duty bands, the comms stream and the four reference cards — takes its shapes from a 2007 World Flight Australia “team 737” crew pack, sent by Nic on 12 September 2026. None of its content survives: that is a different aeroplane, a different era and a different operation, and every figure here is the Longitude's own or is labelled as a convention to agree. What it contributed was four ideas this project did not have — that a block is fired by an event, that two people work in parallel and converge on a checklist, that communications are a stream of their own, and that a monitoring call with the exception in brackets beats a silence that has to be interpreted.

Flows start folded, so the page opens as a checklist-only running order — tap a flow header to open it, tap any line to tick it off. The phase menu jumps to the flow that precedes each checklist and shows where you are; Reset clears the ticks and folds everything away again.

Review (top right) is the editorial view — it numbers every line so changes can be named precisely, and puts the sourcing on show. Flow steps are 13.1, 13.2… and checklist items 13.a, 13.b…, both keyed to the phase number. “Swap 13.2 and 13.6, drop 12.d” is enough to act on. Parallel bands and reference cards carry no numbers — name them by phase and column instead (“the PM column in 12”, “the Monitoring card”).

Review also shows a confidence chip on every line — six cells saying how well sourced it is. The rating is a claim about the source, not about how confident the wording sounds: an invented line written with total assurance still rates red. Each one carries its evidence in the chip’s tooltip and in a data-ev attribute, so a later pass can be argued with rather than guessed at. The point of the scale is what happens when a new source arrives: a step the new source also does moves up, and a step it omits is our bug if we rated it low, or their variation if we rated it Confirmed.
